# Dollar inches higher as 10‑year Treasury yield climbs to highest since 2007

On September 15, 2026 the U.S. 10‑year Treasury yield surged past the 5% mark for the first time since 2007, hitting a high of 5.041% according to Tradeweb data. The rise was driven by heightened tensions in the Middle East, which lifted oil prices and fed inflation concerns, prompting market participants to price in a likely 25‑basis‑point Federal Reserve rate hike at its upcoming policy meeting. The stronger yields boosted the dollar, with the DXY index edging higher, making gold more expensive for holders of other currencies and contributing to the decline in spot gold prices that day.

Analysts noted that the bond market remains “hostage to oil and geopolitics,” with the higher yields reflecting investors’ fears of persistent inflationary pressure from elevated energy costs. The move also signaled confidence among traders that the Fed will continue tightening monetary policy to curb price growth.

Key points:
- 10‑year Treasury yield peaked at 5.041% – highest since 2007.
- Dollar index rose modestly, adding pressure on gold.
- Oil price spikes and Middle‑East tensions drove the rally.
- Markets anticipate a 25‑bp Fed rate hike at the September meeting.
