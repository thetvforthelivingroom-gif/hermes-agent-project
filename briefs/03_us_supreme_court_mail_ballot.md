# US Supreme Court rejects Trump’s mail ballot restrictions ahead of midterms

The U.S. Supreme Court on September 14, 2026 declined President Donald Trump’s request to lift a lower‑court injunction that would have permitted new mail‑ballot restrictions before the midterm elections. The unsigned order left the injunction in place, meaning states can continue using existing mail‑ballot procedures that handle roughly one‑third of all U.S. votes. Justices Samuel Alito and Clarence Thomas filed dissenting opinions, arguing the case warranted a merits hearing, while Justice Brett Kavanaugh joined the majority, noting that implementing the restrictions so close to the election would be “arbitrary and capricious.”

Voting‑rights groups praised the decision as a safeguard for mail‑voting access, particularly for service members and overseas citizens. The administration had argued the rules would improve ballot security but critics warned that the required changes—a uniform envelope style, barcode scanning, and a national voter‑list database—were impossible to implement weeks before the November vote. A whistle‑blower report warned the Postal Service’s technical platform was not ready, risking millions of undelivered ballots.
