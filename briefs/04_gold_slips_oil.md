# Gold slips as oil gains strengthen case for elevated interest rates

On September 15, 2026, gold prices fell as rising crude oil prices heightened inflation concerns and bolstered expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike. Spot gold slipped 0.7% to US$4,266.49 per ounce (RM17,382.52) after hitting a one‑month low on Monday, while US gold futures fell 1% to US$4,307.40. Analysts noted that higher oil prices increase the opportunity cost of holding non‑yielding bullion, reducing its appeal as a hedge against inflation.

The rally in oil pushed benchmark 10‑year U.S. Treasury yields to their highest level since 2007, reinforcing expectations that the Fed would raise rates by a quarter of a percentage point at its upcoming policy meeting. The CME FedWatch Tool indicated a 92% probability of a hike. The dollar also strengthened, making gold more expensive for holders of other currencies.

Key points:
- Spot gold down 0.7% to $4,266.49/oz; US gold futures down 1%.
- Oil price rise contributed to higher inflation expectations.
- 10‑year Treasury yields near 5%, supporting rate‑hike bets.
- Analysts suggest gold would need to climb above $4,440 to ease downside pressure.
