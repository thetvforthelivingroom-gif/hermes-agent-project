# Supreme Court rejects Trump’s mail ballot restrictions for midterm elections

On September 14, 2026 the U.S. Supreme Court issued an unsigned emergency order that left in place a lower‑court injunction blocking President Donald Trump’s request to lift mail‑ballot restrictions ahead of the November midterm elections. The decision preserves the status quo, allowing states to continue using existing mail‑ballot procedures that handle roughly one‑third of all U.S. votes. Justices Samuel Alito and Clarence Thomas dissented, arguing that the case deserved a full merits hearing, while Justice Brett Kavanaugh joined the majority in noting that implementing the restrictions so close to the election would be “arbitrary and capricious.”

Legal analysts note that the Court’s refusal to grant the emergency stay reflects concerns about the logistical feasibility of overhauling the mail‑ballot system weeks before the election. A whistleblower report warned that the proposed changes could lead to millions of undelivered ballots due to technical glitches in the Postal Service’s new portal. Civil‑rights groups praised the ruling as a safeguard for voters, especially overseas and military personnel who rely heavily on mail voting. The administration had argued that the restrictions would improve ballot security, but opponents highlighted the lack of constitutional authority for the President to unilaterally alter election procedures.
