# EXCLUSIVE: Vista Equity exploring strategic options for financial software provider Finastra, sources say - Reuters

## Summary
Private equity firm Vista Equity Partners is conducting an early‑stage strategic review of Finastra, the London‑headquartered provider of core‑banking, payments and lending software used by more than 80 % of the world’s top banks. According to four unnamed sources familiar with the process, Vista has retained Morgan Stanley to advise on possible transactions. The options under consideration include a full sale of Finastra, a partial stake divestment, or a merger with another fintech platform. Sources say the review is still in its “initial phases” and that several investment banks, including Blackstone, have already shown interest.

Finastra was created in 2017 from the merger of Misys and D+H and reported EBITDA of roughly $650 million for the current fiscal year. Valuations are expected in the high‑single‑digit‑billion‑dollar range, with some analysts estimating a top end of $12 billion. Proponents argue that a sale could unlock significant value for Vista’s investors, especially as demand for integrated digital‑banking solutions accelerates worldwide. However, any buyer will need to navigate complex regulatory approvals across the United States, Europe and Asia, assess integration challenges with legacy banking systems, and consider competition from cloud‑native fintech challengers.

The strategic review reflects a broader trend of consolidation in the financial‑software market, where larger players seek to broaden product suites and geographic reach. Recent activity includes Pollen Street Capital’s planned acquisition of Finastra’s core‑banking unit and Vista’s earlier divestiture of the company’s Treasury and Capital Markets division to Apax Partners. Analysts note that the ongoing Iran war and global supply‑chain pressures have heightened interest in technology that can improve operational efficiency for banks.

Overall, the outcome of Vista’s review could reshape the competitive landscape of financial‑software providers, potentially creating a larger, more diversified player capable of challenging both established incumbents and emerging cloud fintechs.
